Tokyo is easier to enjoy when you plan by neighborhood. Instead of crossing the city repeatedly, choose two or three areas that fit together each day and leave room for meals and spontaneous stops.

## Start with your base

Choose accommodation near a useful railway or metro connection. A central address is less important than having an easy route to the areas you want to explore.

## Build neighborhood days

Pair Asakusa with nearby traditional districts, Shibuya with Harajuku, and central museums with quieter streets. This cuts transfer time and leaves more energy for walking.

## Food planning

Tokyo offers everything from casual noodle counters to high-end dining. Do not plan every meal weeks ahead. Reserve only experiences where advance booking genuinely matters.

## What to verify

Rail services, attraction hours and special-event schedules can change. Check official operator and venue websites shortly before travel.

## A balanced first day

After arrival, keep the first day simple: settle in, walk locally, eat something convenient and sleep. Tokyo rewards travelers who start the next morning with energy instead of an overpacked arrival schedule.
