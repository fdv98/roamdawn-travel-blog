A first trip to Japan becomes easier when the route is built around a few bases instead of trying to see the entire country. Tokyo and Kyoto create a strong core; Osaka, Hiroshima or a mountain region can be added when the trip length supports it.

## Days 1–4: Tokyo

Use the first day for arrival, a neighborhood walk and an early night if needed. Then group Tokyo by area: traditional streets and temples on one day, western neighborhoods and shopping on another, and museums or a slower local day on the third.

## Days 5–7: Kyoto

Travel to Kyoto and avoid overloading each day with distant temples. Group the eastern side, central sights and Arashiyama into separate blocks. Early starts are useful at popular sites.

## Days 8–10: Choose your extension

With three extra days, choose Osaka for food and urban energy, Hiroshima for history, or simply add more time to Kyoto and Tokyo. The best extension is the one that does not turn the final days into constant transit.

## Planning tips

Reserve only experiences that genuinely need advance planning. Keep at least one flexible half-day for weather or a neighborhood you discover along the way. Check official transport information shortly before departure because schedules and services can change.
